The problem

The same plot looks very different once you check the facts

It depends on what grid capacity is actually available, whether the water basin is under stress, or how many competing facilities are already queued for permits nearby. Today these are scored once, at acquisition, by a consultant, and the answer is out of date soon after.

Investors

Price assets on data that is already out of date by the time it reaches the investment committee.

Developers

Commit land and permits before the binding constraint, usually grid power, is visible.

Sustainability teams

Grid mix, cooling climate and water stress set a site’s carbon and resource footprint for decades.

The cost of the gap

Undetected risk becomes a stranded asset

The stranded-asset situation in Ireland was not a failure of ambition or capital. It was a failure to track one risk factor, grid capacity, continuously, before it became a binding constraint. When the grid operator froze new connections, projects that looked viable at acquisition had no path to power.
