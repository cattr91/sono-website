The problem

An alarm tells you something is wrong. It does not tell you where, how much it matters, or who it hits.

Today the link between a BMS signal, the equipment behind it and the customers it serves lives in manually built Excel mappings and in the heads of individual operators. Priority rests on the experience of whoever is on shift. Shift staff are scarce, rotate often and rarely have the full context.

Operators

Face hundreds of unprioritised alarms and decide from memory which one matters.

Shift teams

High rotation and little context. A new technician cannot know which rack sits behind which signal.

Customers

Contracts require prompt notice of impact, yet nobody can say quickly which customer a failing device serves.

Sustainability teams

Late or wrong actions waste energy and trigger the emergency, carbon-intensive outcome.

The cost of the gap

The alarm that mattered was one of 200

During a maintenance handover at a hyperscale site, more than 200 unprioritised alarms were open when a UPS began running down its battery. The one that mattered was not distinguishable from the rest. The battery lasted five minutes, and about 3 MW went dark for 20 minutes. Origin, priority and affected customer would have answered the technician’s first three questions.

Product

One alarm, three answers.

Sono reads the signals your BMS and EPMS already produce and the asset data you already hold, and attaches to every alarm what an operator needs to act.

Where does it come from?

Exact origin

The controller and point the signal comes from, the equipment it belongs to, and where that equipment sits in the plant. The mapping that is manual today becomes a maintained record.

What do I handle first?

Computed priority

Your alarm classes set the severity. Sono calculates how urgent the alarm is and what impact it has, and combines them, so priority no longer depends on who is on shift.

Who is affected?

Full flow

From the signal to the equipment, the system it serves, the rack and the customer. The affected customer can be alerted immediately, which most contracts require anyway.

What we deliberately do not build: a digital twin of the data centre, a CMMS or a ticketing system. We need enough information to trace a signal to its exact source and down to the racks and clients, and we work with the systems you already run.

How it works

The map from signal to customer should not live in an Excel sheet or in one operator’s head

Today the mapping from signal to equipment to rack to customer is built by hand in Excel. Sono turns it into a maintained record and puts it behind every alarm.

Step 1

Connect

Sono reads your BMS or EPMS point list and your asset data from your CMMS, as a one-time file upload or through an API.

Step 2

Map

Signals, equipment and racks are structured into three linked sheets, so every signal can be followed from its source to the customer. An optional pre-analysis fills the gaps with your team.

Step 3

Act

Operators get alarms ordered by priority, with origin and affected customers attached. A person approves every ticket and every customer alert.

Integration

Built on the systems you already run

Sono connects to your BMS and EPMS and to your CMMS or ticketing tool. Your data stays in your network, and a person approves every action.
